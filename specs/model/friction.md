# Friction

## Purpose

The viscous pressure gradient in the momentum balance, and the Darcy friction factor that scales it.

## Interface

| Function | Inputs | Output | Symbolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| viscous pressure gradient | $f_D$, $D$ (m), $\rho_m$ (kg/m³), $v_m$ (m/s) | $F$ (Pa/m) | yes |
| friction factor | per option; none for FRIC-2 | $f_D$ (–) | yes, for options that depend on the state |

In `develop` the friction model is a component of the well, a `FrictionModel` whose `pressure_gradient(s, fluid, D)` gives $F$ at a point's state $s$; the fluid model supplies the viscosities. The options are `FixedFrictionFactor(f_D)` (FRIC-2) and `RoughnessFriction(roughness, correlation)` (FRIC-3 with FRIC-4 or FRIC-5, and FRIC-6).

## Equations

### FRIC-1 · Viscous pressure gradient

Darcy–Weisbach for the mixture:

$$F = \frac{f_D}{2D}\,\rho_m\, v_m \lvert v_m\rvert$$

v1.0.0 implements $\rho_m v_m^2$, which is the same for $v_m \ge 0$; every admissible state has $v_m > 0$ (SOL-1).

### FRIC-2 · Fixed friction factor

$f_D$ is a constant parameter of the well, the same in every cell. Used by `v1.0.0`.

### FRIC-3 · Reynolds number

$$\text{Re} = \frac{\rho_m\,\lvert v_m\rvert\, D}{\mu_m}$$

with the mixture viscosity $\mu_m$ of PVT-MIX-9 at the point, and $f_D$ from FRIC-6 at this Re and the relative roughness $\varepsilon/D$, where $\varepsilon > 0$ is the pipe wall's roughness (m).

### FRIC-4 · Chen friction factor

Chen (1979), an explicit approximation of the Colebrook–White equation for turbulent flow, as used by Hasan, Kabir and Sayarpour (2010), Eqs. (A-4) and (A-5):

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{f_t}} = -2\log_{10}\!\left[\frac{\varepsilon/D}{3.7065} - \frac{5.0452}{\text{Re}}\,\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{(\varepsilon/D)^{1.1098}}{2.8257} + \left(\frac{7.149}{\text{Re}}\right)^{0.8981}\right)\right]$$

Within about 0.5% of Colebrook–White.

### FRIC-5 · Haaland friction factor

Haaland (1983), an alternative to FRIC-4:

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{f_t}} = -1.8\log_{10}\!\left[\left(\frac{\varepsilon/D}{3.7}\right)^{1.11} + \frac{6.9}{\text{Re}}\right]$$

Within about 1.5% of Colebrook–White.

### FRIC-6 · Laminar–turbulent blend

$$f_D = (1 - s)\,\frac{64}{\text{Re}_1} + s\, f_t(\text{Re}_2), \qquad s = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-0.005\,(\text{Re}_1 - 3000)}},$$

with $\text{Re}_1 = \operatorname{smax}(\text{Re}, 1)$, $\text{Re}_2 = \operatorname{smax}(\text{Re}_1, 1000)$ (SMO-1, $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$), and $f_t$ the turbulent friction factor of FRIC-4 or FRIC-5. The sigmoid (SMO-4) blends the laminar $64/\text{Re}$ into the turbulent value around Re = 3000, over a width of about 1000; the smooth maxima keep both branches finite at small Re.

## Options

| Option | IDs | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed $f_D$ | FRIC-1, FRIC-2 | `v1.0.0`; `develop`'s `FixedFrictionFactor` |
| $f_D$ from the Reynolds number and the pipe roughness, Chen | FRIC-1, FRIC-3, FRIC-4, FRIC-6 | `develop` default: `RoughnessFriction()`, $\varepsilon = 4.5\cdot10^{-5}$ m (commercial steel) |
| The same, Haaland | FRIC-1, FRIC-3, FRIC-5, FRIC-6 | `develop`: `RoughnessFriction(correlation='haaland')` |

## Safeguards

None for FRIC-2. FRIC-6's smooth maxima keep Re above about 1 in the laminar branch and above about 1000 in the turbulent one. `FixedFrictionFactor` raises `ValueError` unless $f_D > 0$, and `RoughnessFriction` unless $\varepsilon > 0$ and the correlation is one of the two.
