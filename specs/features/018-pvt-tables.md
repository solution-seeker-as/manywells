# 018 · PVT tables, and one interface for every PVT model

*Feature spec, 2026-10-28. Status: approved by Bjarne on 2026-10-28, with each decision as recommended (Rulings). Phase A, the interface (ruling 1), is implemented: `FluidModel(pvt=...)` with `DeadOil` and `BlackOil`, each method's value unchanged to the bit. Phase B, the tables in Python and the CasADi backend (`specs/model/pvt/table.md`, PVT-TAB-1 to PVT-TAB-7), phase C, the tables in the Rust core, and phase D, the measurements on both backends (acceptance 9, Measurements), the guide `docs/pvt_tables.md` and the reworded non-goal (ruling 2), are implemented. The prototype is in `plans/evidence/` (Measurements) and the example tables in `tests/data/pvt/`. The bug the prototype found is issue #10, fixed by PR #23.*

## Rulings (Bjarne, 2026-10-28)

Each decision as recommended; the sections below give the reasons and the measurements.

1. **The interface.** The hydrocarbon PVT becomes a component of `FluidModel`, `pvt`, with one class per option: `DeadOil`, `BlackOil` (Vazquez–Beggs) and `PVTTable`, behind an abstract `PVTModel`. `FluidModel` keeps the stream (water, the ratios, the heat capacities) and the mixing. This breaks the API: `oil_model`, `ideal_gas`, `z_factor_model`, `p_sep`, `T_sep`, `p_bubble`, `rho_o` and `rho_g` move into the PVT classes (Breaking change). Rejected: keeping `FluidModel`'s flat fields and adding `oil_model='table'` with a `table` field, a smaller diff, but every new option would add fields that the other options ignore.
2. **Equations of state enter as tables.** ManyWells holds no equation of state and no flash: the non-goal stands. The library converts a flash's phase split into a table (PVT-TAB-6); the flash is the user's (NeqSim, PVTsim, a PVT report). An equation of state called at run time is left for a later spec (design choice 7); until then it can be a user's `PVTModel` subclass on the CasADi backend only, as for any component (architecture, decision 1). The constitution's non-goal is reworded (and `specs/goals.md`'s with it): "Compositional PVT: cubic equations of state and flash calculations. ManyWells stays with black oil, correlations and tables; a fluid from an equation of state enters as a table (feature 018)."
3. **The interpolation** is bilinear in $(p, T)$ on the table's own grid, with $p$ and $T$ clamped to it. The simulator refuses boundary conditions whose $p_r$ or $T_r$ lies outside the grid (`ValueError`), and a `Root` records whether any of its points lies off the grid, with a warning on the `manywells` logger for an operating point that does. No cubic interpolation and no refinement step (design choices 1 to 3).
4. **What a table holds:** $R_{so}$, $B_o$ and $Z$ at every node, optionally $\mu_o$, $\mu_g$ and $\sigma_{go}$, and the stock-tank densities $\rho_o$ and $\rho_{g,\text{sc}}$ they refer to (PVT-TAB-1). $Z$, not $\rho_g$, is tabulated (design choice 2).
5. **A GOR axis, read in Python.** A table may hold the fluid at several GORs, each on its own $(p, T)$ grid. `FluidModel` reads it at its own `gor` when it is built, linearly in GOR between the two neighbouring GORs (PVT-TAB-7), so both backends see one $(p, T)$ table, as GEO-4 interpolates a survey in Python. A table with one GOR serves other GORs by the black-oil rule (PVT-OIL-13), which on the synthetic fluid errs by 1.2 to 1.7% in no-slip density at 10% from the table's GOR and by 2.4 to 3.8% at 20 to 25%; the axis cuts that to 0.01 to 0.3% (design choice 5). Calibration rows differ in GOR, so this matters there most.
6. **Temperature.** A table may have one temperature; its properties then do not depend on $T$. The docs say what that costs: on sampled wells, PWH 0.9 bar higher at the median and up to 7.9 bar (design choice 4). Scaling a one-temperature table by Vazquez–Beggs's temperature dependence is a later option of its own.
7. **Undersaturated oil** (`specs/features/007-black-oil.md`, the open item "Dissolved gas in the density"): a table follows whatever the black oil's rule is. At the table's own GOR the conflict does not arise (design choice 6).
8. **Example tables:** four, all generated by scripts in this repository from develop's black oil or a synthetic composition (`tests/data/pvt/`). No third-party data: SPE1's black-oil tables (Odeh 1981) are under the Open Database License in OPM's decks and were left out.
9. **The CasADi search with an all-liquid bottomhole is fixed first** (Findings 1; issue #10), as a change of its own on `develop`: the march's retry of point 0, which Bjarne chose on 2026-10-28 (PR #23). The CasADi search misses 13% of such operating points before it, with develop's black oil, and tables make them common. With it, it finds all of them on 263 sampled cases at three GORs, for 0.16% more Ipopt iterations, and leaves the verifier's root sets unchanged.

Three details of phase B, each as recommended (Bjarne, 2026-10-28):

10. **$R_{so}$ nondecreasing in $p$, to rounding.** PVT-TAB-1's check accepts a fall of at most $10^{-12}\max(1, \lvert R_{so}\rvert)$. PVT-TAB-7's arithmetic makes falls of about $10^{-14}$ where $R_{so}$ is flat above a bubble point, as a user's own tool may. Rejected: an exact check, with `at_gor` forcing its result monotone by a running maximum.
11. **A column only one of two neighbouring GOR tables has is dropped** from the table between them (PVT-TAB-7), and the correlations stand in for it (PVT-TAB-5). Rejected: refusing an axis whose tables differ in their columns.
12. **`plans/evidence/pvt_table_eos.py` stays where it is**, with the measurements of the same fluid. It needs NeqSim and Java; `AGENTS.md`'s table of reference data points to it. `specs/tools/make_pvt_tables.py` writes the two tables ManyWells makes itself. Rejected: splitting the table writing into `specs/tools/`.

## Motivation

- **The black oil's shape is wrong between its end points.** Vazquez–Beggs with one gas gravity sets $R_{so}(p)$ and $B_o(p)$ from the API and gas gravities alone. Bjarne's validation against a tuned equation of state of a real well (private data; only this qualitative finding is public) found that the black oil's phase split along the tubing, not only its value at the bubble point, is a lever of several bar on the bottomhole-to-wellhead pressure drop, as large as the slip closure; matching $R_{so}$ and $B_o$ at the bubble point recovered only part of it. Its recommendation 1: let the black oil take the fluid's measured $R_{so}$ and $B_o$, or a PVT table. The synthetic fluid of the Measurements shows the same on sampled wells, with public data only.
- **Tables are what users have.** A PVT report gives $R_{so}$, $B_o$, $B_g$ and the viscosities against pressure at the reservoir temperature; PVT programs write $(p, T)$ tables for flow simulators. Neither can enter ManyWells today.
- **The PVT module has no interface to fill in.** `FluidModel` selects its options by switches, and `p_sep`, `T_sep` and `p_bubble` mean nothing to dead oil. Every other component with options (friction, inflow, choke) has one class per option. A table, a matched black oil or a user's equation of state would each add more switches to one class.

## Delta

### The interface (`specs/architecture.md`)

`FluidModel` keeps its methods and their contract (the Components table of `specs/architecture.md` is unchanged), and gets the hydrocarbon properties from its `pvt` component:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FluidModel:
    pvt: PVTModel = BlackOil()  # the hydrocarbon PVT
    rho_w: float = 999.1  # water, incompressible (PVT-WAT-1)
    gor: float = 200.0
    wlr: float = 0.0
    cp_g, cp_o, cp_w  # as now
    surface_tension_model: str = 'oil'
    # rho_o, rho_g: properties, from pvt
```

| `PVTModel` member | Inputs | Output | `DeadOil` | `BlackOil` | `PVTTable` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rho_o`, `rho_g` | – | kg/m³ at standard conditions | field | field | the table's |
| `dissolves_gas` | – | bool | false: INF-5's rates, exactly | true | true |
| `rs(p, T)`, `bo(p, T)` | bar, K | Sm³/Sm³, – | 0, 1 | PVT-OIL-6 to PVT-OIL-8 | PVT-TAB-3 |
| `gas_density(p, T)`, `gas_law_row(p, T, rho_g)` | bar, K, kg/m³ | kg/m³; bar | `gas_law`: PVT-GAS-1, PVT-GAS-3 or PVT-GAS-11 | the same | PVT-TAB-4 |
| `jt_factor(T, rho_g)` | K, kg/m³ | – | 0 for an ideal gas, else PVT-GAS-10 | the same | PVT-GAS-10 |
| `oil_viscosity(p, T)` | bar, K | Pa s | PVT-OIL-10 | PVT-OIL-10, PVT-OIL-11 | PVT-TAB-5 |
| `gas_viscosity(p, T, rho_g)` | bar, K, kg/m³ | Pa s | PVT-GAS-7 | PVT-GAS-7 | PVT-TAB-5 |
| `oil_surface_tension(p, T)` | bar, K | N/m | PVT-OIL-3 at $\rho_o$ | with PVT-OIL-12 | PVT-TAB-5 |
| `pseudo_critical` | – | (Pa, K) | PVT-GAS-5 of $\rho_g$ | the same | the same |

- **What stays in `FluidModel`:** the stream at standard conditions (PVT-MIX-10), the reservoir gas rate (INF-4), the phase rates (INF-5, or PVT-OIL-13 where `pvt.dissolves_gas`), the liquid density (PVT-MIX-1 or PVT-MIX-6), the liquid and mixture viscosities (PVT-MIX-8, PVT-MIX-9) and the surface-tension option (PVT-MIX-5, PVT-MIX-7). Oil and water mix by in-situ volume (PVT-MIX-6 and PVT-MIX-8, issue #8), with a table's $B_o$ as with the black oil's, and the water stays incompressible ($B_w = 1$): recommendation 1's other half is on `develop` already.
- **`gas_law`** replaces `ideal_gas` and `z_factor_model`: `'ideal'`, `'dak'` (default) or `'papay'`. Two fields with one meaning become one.
- **Rows.** No row changes form. The gas-law row of a table is PVT-GAS-3's with the table's $Z$ (PVT-TAB-4); `discretization.row_ids` gets the gas law's ID from the PVT model.
- **Extension points.** The Fluid row of the extension-point table becomes "a `PVTModel` class (an oil model with its gas law), or a mass-transfer law behind `FluidModel.phase_rates`". A user's subclass runs on the CasADi backend only.
- **The Rust core.** One enum, `Pvt::{DeadOil, BlackOil, Table}`, replaces `OilModel` and `GasLaw` in `Fluid`, dispatched by `match` (design point 1). A table's grid and columns are arrays, so `FluidInputs` and `Fluid` stop being `Copy`; the table is shared by `Arc`. `solvers/rust.py` maps the three classes, and refuses others with `ValueError`.

### `specs/model/pvt/table.md` (new)

**PVT-TAB-1 · The table.** A grid $p_1 < \dots < p_m$ (bar, $m \ge 2$) and $T_1 < \dots < T_n$ (K, $n \ge 1$), every node $(p_j, T_k)$ present, with $R_{so}$ (Sm³/Sm³), $B_o$ (–) and $Z$ (–) at each, and optionally $\mu_o$, $\mu_g$ (Pa s) and $\sigma_{go}$ (N/m); and the densities at standard conditions $\rho_o$ and $\rho_{g,\text{sc}}$ (kg/m³) that they refer to.

- **Meaning, by the role in the balances.** Per Sm³ of stock-tank oil, the oil at $(p, T)$ carries the mass $\rho_o + R_{so}\rho_{g,\text{sc}}$ in the volume $B_o$ (PVT-OIL-9), and the free gas has the density $c_\text{bar}\thinspace p/(Z R_s T)$ with $R_s = p_\text{ref}/(\rho_{g,\text{sc}}T_\text{ref})$ (PVT-GAS-2). A table made from a flash may therefore have $R_{so} < 0$ and $B_o < 1$ where the free gas carries components that are oil at standard conditions (PVT-TAB-6). Above the fluid's bubble point $R_{so}$ is the table's GOR, and $Z$ is that of the gas that would appear first, so that the column is continuous.
- **Checks** (`ValueError` when the table is built): finite values; strictly increasing grids; $B_o > 0$, $Z > 0$, $\mu_o, \mu_g, \sigma_{go} > 0$; $R_{so}$ nondecreasing in $p$ at every $T$; and $R_{so} \ge 0$ unless the table has $\mu_o$ and $\sigma_{go}$, since the live-oil corrections PVT-OIL-11 and PVT-OIL-12 are undefined below $R_{so} = -17.8$ Sm³/Sm³ and untested below 0.
- **GOR axis** (optional). The table may hold the stream at GORs $R_1 < \dots < R_l$, each with a rectangular grid of its own, all with the same $\rho_o$ and $\rho_{g,\text{sc}}$: the stream recombined from the same separator gas and oil, so that every GOR is on the same tie line at standard conditions. Without the axis, the table's GOR is free text, for the reader.
- **File.** CSV: `# key: value` lines (`rho_o`, `rho_g`, and free-text `name`, `source`, `separation`, `gor`), then the columns `[gor,] p, T, R_so, B_o, Z[, mu_o, mu_g, sigma]`, one row per node, in Sm³/Sm³, bar, K and SI units. `PVTTable.read_csv` and `write_csv`; field units and other formats (Eclipse `PVTO`/`PVDG`, OLGA `.tab`) are converted outside ManyWells.

**PVT-TAB-2 · Interpolation.** A column $y$ at $(p, T)$ is $\hat y(\tilde p, \tilde T)$, the bilinear interpolation on the grid at $\tilde p = \min(\max(p, p_1), p_m)$ and $\tilde T = \min(\max(T, T_1), T_n)$; with $n = 1$, linear in $\tilde p$ and independent of $T$. It is exact at the nodes and continuous, and its derivatives jump at grid lines. The CasADi backend uses `casadi.interpolant(..., 'linear', ...)` on the clamped inputs, one call node per column and point; the Rust core the same arithmetic, so that the two agree to rounding (component vectors at $10^{-12}$).

**PVT-TAB-3 · Oil.** $R_{so} = \hat R_{so}(p, T)$ and $B_o = \hat B_o(p, T)$, in PVT-OIL-9, PVT-MIX-6 and PVT-OIL-13 as for the black oil.

**PVT-TAB-4 · Gas law.** $c_\text{bar}\thinspace p = \hat Z(p, T)\rho_g R_s T$, with the row $p - \rho_g\hat Z R_s T/c_\text{bar}$ (bar), PVT-GAS-3's form. The density at $(p, T)$ is explicit: no Newton solve, unlike PVT-GAS-11. The Joule–Thomson factor is DAK's (PVT-GAS-10) with the pseudo-critical properties of $\rho_{g,\text{sc}}$, as for every real gas (016's ruling).

**PVT-TAB-5 · Viscosities and surface tension.** $\mu_o = \hat\mu_o(p, T)$, $\mu_g = \hat\mu_g(p, T)$ and $\sigma_{go} = \hat\sigma_{go}(p, T)$ where the table has them; otherwise PVT-OIL-10 and PVT-OIL-11 with the API gravity of $\rho_o$ and the table's $R_{so}$, PVT-GAS-7 at the state's $(T, \rho_g)$, and PVT-OIL-3 at $\rho_o$ with PVT-OIL-12. They enter PVT-MIX-7 to PVT-MIX-9 as now. $\hat\mu_g$ is read at the point's pressure, not at its gas density.

**PVT-TAB-6 · A table from a flash.** Python only; no row uses it. Given the stream's separation (stock-tank oil density $\rho_o$, gas density at standard conditions $\rho_{g,\text{sc}}$ and GOR $R_{go}$) and, at each node, the flash's vapour mass fraction $X$ of the hydrocarbon stream and the phase densities $\rho_V$ and $\rho_L$, with $m = \rho_o + R_{go}\rho_{g,\text{sc}}$:

$$R_{so} = R_{go} - \frac{X m}{\rho_{g,\text{sc}}}, \qquad B_o = \frac{(1 - X) m}{\rho_L}, \qquad Z = \frac{c_\text{bar}\thinspace p}{\rho_V R_s T}.$$

At the GOR $R_{go}$, PVT-TAB-3 and PVT-TAB-4 then give the flash's phase mass rates and phase densities exactly at the nodes: without water or lift gas, PVT-OIL-13 gives $w_g/(w_g + w_l) = (R_{go} - R_{so})\rho_{g,\text{sc}}/m = X$. `PVTTable.from_phase_split(p, T, X, rho_L, rho_V, rho_o, rho_g, gor, ...)` implements it. Where the stream is all liquid, the caller supplies $\rho_V$ of the gas that would appear first (PVT-TAB-1).

**PVT-TAB-7 · The table at the fluid's GOR.** Python only, when `FluidModel` is built. With a GOR axis and the fluid's GOR $R_{go}$ in $[R_i, R_{i+1}]$, each of the two GORs' columns is interpolated bilinearly (PVT-TAB-2) onto the union of their two grids, and the result is $(1 - w)\hat y_i + w\hat y_{i+1}$ with $w = (R_{go} - R_i)/(R_{i+1} - R_i)$: a $(p, T)$ table whose GOR is $R_{go}$, exact at the axis's GORs. A GOR outside $[R_1, R_l]$ raises `ValueError`. `dataclasses.replace(fluid, gor=...)`, which calibration does for every row, reads the axis again. The Rust core receives the resulting table and never sees the axis.

## Design choices

1. **Bilinear, not cubic.** On 36 cases of 12 sampled wells (part `interp`), a table on a 10 bar by 10 K grid, interpolated bilinearly or by CasADi's C2 cubic B-spline, finds an operating point in the same 33 cases as the black oil. The kinks do not cost Ipopt iterations: 11,138 in total against the black oil's 11,319 and the B-spline's 12,303. The B-spline is more accurate on that grid (PBH within $3\cdot10^{-5}$ bar, against 0.011 bar), but slower (303 s in total, against 233 s for bilinear and 147 s for the black oil, on 4 processes), returns 0 off its grid, and its fit would have to be ported to Rust. Bilinear interpolation is one formula in both backends, keeps a bubble-point node's kink exactly, and its error falls with the grid spacing, which the table's maker chooses.
2. **$Z$, not $\rho_g$.** The gas density is nearly proportional to $p$; $Z$ varies slowly. On the 10 bar by 10 K grid, interpolating $Z$ gives the gas density within 0.21% of the black oil's on 40 sampled fluids, and interpolating $\rho_g$ within 2.1% (part `points`). With $Z$ the gas density also keeps scaling with $p$ where $p$ is clamped.
3. **Clamped, and refused outside the grid.** A trial state of the march or of Ipopt can lie far outside the well's pressures and temperatures; clamping keeps every property finite and positive there, where linear extrapolation can make $B_o$ or $Z$ negative. A root off the grid rests on clamped values. The bottom of the well is known before the solve, so $p_r$ or $T_r$ outside the grid is refused; the rest of the well is not, since the fluid stays far warmer than the ambient $T_s$ (277.2 K at every sampled well, below `synthetic_eos_oil.csv`'s 280 K) and its wellhead pressure is an output. So the root carries the flag instead, which the Measurements count. No refinement step in the model: refining a 25 bar by 25 K table to 2 bar by 5 K by monotone cubics (Fritsch–Carlson) before the bilinear step cuts its error where the fluid is smooth (largest $R_{so}$ error 0.58 against 3.2 Sm³/Sm³; on the sampled wells, whose black oil has no bubble point, largest PBH change 0.018 against 0.059 bar), but rounds off the kink at a bubble point (2.2 Sm³/Sm³ against 0.50, with a node there). Whether refining helps depends on the fluid, which the table's maker knows and the model does not; a maker with a coarse table can refine it before writing it.
4. **Temperature.** A table at the reservoir temperature alone, against the black oil it was made from, raises PWH by 0.9 bar at the median, 3.5 bar at the 90th percentile and 7.9 bar at most, on 252 sampled cases (Measurements): at the colder wellhead the table frees too much gas and swells the oil too much, so the column is too light. One-temperature tables stay allowed, because PVT reports give one, and the docs say what they cost; a table for a well should span its temperatures.
5. **A GOR axis, read in Python.** A row's GOR need not be the table's. Without an axis, the black-oil rule reads the table: gas dissolves up to the smaller of $R_{so}$ and the GOR, the rest is free at the table's $Z$, and the oil keeps the table's $B_o$. That is wrong in two ways: above the table's bubble point its $R_{so}$ stops at the table's GOR, while a richer stream dissolves more; and the oil's composition, so its density and its gas's, changes with the GOR. The synthetic fluid, recombined from its own separator gas and oil at 0.6 to 2 times its GOR (`pvt_table_eos.py gor`), measures both (Measurements): within 10% of the table's GOR the no-slip density errs by 1.2 to 1.7% at the median and 4.7% at most; at 20 to 25%, by 2.4 to 3.8% and 11%. Interpolating linearly in GOR between tables 10 to 33% apart errs by 0.01 to 0.3% at the median, and by 1 to 2% at most, next to a bubble point that is a node of neither table. The axis costs neither backend anything, because the GOR is a constant of the fluid: PVT-TAB-7 runs once, in Python, like GEO-4's survey interpolation. Each GOR keeps its own grid, so that each has its own bubble points as nodes; a common grid would have to hold every GOR's bubble point at every temperature.
6. **Undersaturated oil.** PVT-MIX-6 evaluates the live-oil density at $R_{so}(p, T)$ even where the stream carries less gas (007's open item "Dissolved gas in the density"). At a table's own GOR, $R_{so} \le R_{go}$ everywhere, since $R_{so}$ is $R_{go}$ at and above the bubble point; a GOR axis reads the table at the fluid's GOR, so the conflict arises only for a table without the axis, at rows below its GOR. Whichever rule Bjarne chooses for that item applies to tables too.
7. **The interface as one ABC with three classes.** It costs nothing in the rows: the methods are those `FluidModel` already calls. An equation of state called at run time is left for a later spec. The interface does not exclude it: on the CasADi backend a flash can be a `casadi.Callback` inside the rows, with finite-difference derivatives; the Rust core has no way to call one today (architecture, decision 1). Until then an equation of state reaches ManyWells through PVT-TAB-6.

## Off in the `v1.0.0` configuration

- `v1_well` builds `FluidModel(pvt=DeadOil(rho_o=rho_l, rho_g=..., gas_law='ideal'), ...)`; `configurations.check` flags any other class or gas law. A table is never in the configuration.
- The refactor moves code and changes no equation: the row vectors, the develop vectors and both verifier reports are unchanged.

## Measurements

The prototype (`plans/evidence/pvt_tables.py`) adds a table by a subclass of `FluidModel`, so it runs on the CasADi backend only. Its tables are made from each well's own black oil at the nodes, so that a table differs from the black oil by its interpolation alone. Everything here was measured on `develop` at `8b4f1e6`, before issue #8's in-situ mixing of oil and water (merged in `ae754d8`). A table and the black oil it is compared with mix the same way, so the interpolation's errors do not depend on the mixing; the equation-of-state fluid's differences from the black oil would move a little in wells with water. Grids span 1 to 470 bar and 270 to 430 K; the sampler draws $p_r$ up to 457 bar and temperatures from 277 to 423 K.

**Interpolation error** (part `points`): the largest error over 2000 random points in 5–460 bar and 277–423 K, on each of 40 sampled fluids; the largest over the fluids, and the median of the fluids' largest.

| fine, 2 bar by 5 K | 0.13 / 0.05 | 0.015 / 0.010 | 0.008 / 0.004 | 0.07 / 0.03 |
| medium, 10 bar by 10 K | 0.45 / 0.20 | 0.06 / 0.04 | 0.030 / 0.015 | 0.21 / 0.10 |
| coarse, 25 bar by 25 K | 3.2 / 1.3 | 0.36 / 0.25 | 0.19 / 0.09 | 1.5 / 0.67 |

## Findings

1. **The CasADi search misses operating points where the bottomhole is all liquid** (issue #10; `plans/improvements.md` §1.7, a cause of §2.9). Where all the gas is dissolved at the bottomhole, the march's guess for point 0, a void fraction of 0.5, is far from the root's $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-3}$. Point 0 then fails at every start near the root, and the search finds none. develop's own black oil has the gap: on the 263 sampled cases at their GOR and at half and a quarter of it, the CasADi search misses 15 of the 116 operating points with an all-liquid bottomhole, which the Rust core finds (at `ae754d8`). A table with the fluid's bubble point makes such bottomholes common: 16 of the 17 operating points the prototype's table lost were such cases. At well 60 of seed 2026, for example, it finds one at 308.3 bar once 0.01 kg/s of lift gas keeps some gas free. The fix, which Bjarne chose: the march retries point 0 from the phase rates' no-slip void fraction where it fails from the guess. It finds all 116 and loses none, for 0.16% more Ipopt iterations, and leaves the verifier's root sets unchanged to the bit (§1.7; PR #23). It comes before acceptance 9 on the CasADi backend (decision 9).
2. **CasADi's B-spline interpolant is 0 outside its grid** (`casadi.interpolant(..., 'bspline', ...)`, casadi 3.8.1), where the linear one extrapolates. Any use of it on states that can leave the grid must clamp its inputs.

## Breaking change

For the v2 CHANGELOG:

```python
# before
FluidModel(rho_o=850, rho_g=0.8, gor=150, wlr=0.2, p_bubble=180, z_factor_model='papay')
FluidModel(rho_o=850, rho_g=0.8, gor=150, oil_model='dead_oil', ideal_gas=True)
# after
FluidModel(pvt=BlackOil(rho_o=850, rho_g=0.8, p_bubble=180, gas_law='papay'), gor=150, wlr=0.2)
FluidModel(pvt=DeadOil(rho_o=850, rho_g=0.8, gas_law='ideal'), gor=150)
FluidModel(pvt=PVTTable.read_csv('fluid.csv'), gor=150, wlr=0.2)
```

`fluid.rho_o`, `fluid.rho_g`, `fluid.rs(p, T)` and the other methods keep working. A dataset's config table records the PVT class and its fields in place of the flat fields; the sampler draws only `BlackOil`, so its datasets carry no tables.

## Out of scope

- **An equation of state or a flash in ManyWells**, and one called at run time (non-goal; decision 2 and design choice 7; a later spec).
- **Tables that vary with the water cut**, and a GOR axis in the backends: PVT-TAB-7 reads it in Python.
- **Water from a table** ($B_w \ne 1$, the water's viscosity). Oil and water already mix by in-situ volume (issue #8).
- **A matched black oil:** Vazquez–Beggs scaled to a measured $R_{so}$ and $B_o$ at the bubble point, or a one-temperature table extended in $T$ by Vazquez–Beggs's temperature dependence. A natural next option of `BlackOil` once the interface exists.
- **Readers for other formats** (Eclipse `PVTO`/`PVDG`, OLGA `.tab`, PVTsim's JSON). A script converts them to the CSV.
- **Tables in the sampler** (`specs/sampling.md` is unchanged), and enthalpies, heat capacities or Joule–Thomson factors from a table.
- **The fix of 007's open item, dissolved gas in the density** (design choice 6).
